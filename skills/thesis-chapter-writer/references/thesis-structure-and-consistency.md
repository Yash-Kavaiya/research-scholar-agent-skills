# Thesis structure, terminology control, and consistency

## 1. Thesis formats

| Format | Structure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Monograph | Introduction; literature review; methodology; results or study chapters; discussion; conclusion | The traditional format; one continuous argument |
| Thesis by publication | Introduction (exegesis); published or submitted papers as chapters; linking text; discussion; conclusion | Rules govern co-authorship statements, the number of papers, and permissions |
| Hybrid | Monograph chapters with some chapters based on papers | Common in science and engineering |

Follow the university's regulations for format, length, and front matter (declaration, abstract, acknowledgements, contents, lists of figures and tables, abbreviations).

## 2. Chapter roles and their sections

| Chapter | Sections | Length guide (varies widely) |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Background; problem statement; aims, objectives, research questions; scope and delimitations; significance; contributions; outline | 10% |
| Literature review | Thematic synthesis leading to the gap | 15–25% |
| Methodology | Design, participants or data, instruments, procedure, analysis, quality, ethics | 10–15% |
| Study chapters | Introduction; specific methods; results; chapter discussion; summary | 30–45% in total |
| Discussion | Findings against the questions; interpretation against the literature; implications; limitations | 10–15% |
| Conclusion | Answers; contributions; limitations; future work; closing | 5% |

## 3. The thesis map

Keep a one-page document that lists the central argument and, per chapter, the question it addresses, its contribution, and how it links to the next. Revise it whenever a chapter's role changes. Each chapter introduction and summary should agree with it.

## 4. Terminology sheet

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Preferred term | The one form used |
| Definition | With source if borrowed |
| First defined | Chapter and section |
| Avoid | Synonyms and variants not to use |
| Abbreviation | Defined at first use in each chapter if the university requires |
| Notation | Symbols with meaning and type |

Also record: spelling variant, capitalisation of method and dataset names, hyphenation choices, number style, and tense conventions.

## 5. Chapter introductions and summaries

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Chapter introduction | Where we are in the thesis; the question this chapter addresses; how it builds on the previous chapter; the structure of the chapter |
| Chapter summary | What was established; how it answers its question; what the next chapter does with it |

These signposts are expected in theses even though papers omit them.

## 6. Thesis depth compared with a paper

Add in the thesis what a paper compresses: reasons for each design choice and the alternatives rejected; full procedures; negative and secondary results; detailed definitions for readers outside the subfield; extended discussion of limitations.

## 7. Adapting a published paper into a chapter

1. Remove the paper's abstract, introduction, and related work, or reduce them to a short chapter introduction, since the thesis introduction and review cover them.
2. Restore detail that the page limit removed.
3. Replace terms and notation with those on the terminology sheet.
4. Add cross-references to other chapters.
5. Add the co-authorship and prior-publication statement the university requires, describing the candidate's contribution.
6. Check the publisher's policy on reuse in a thesis.
7. Write a chapter summary linking to the next chapter.

## 8. Cross-referencing

- Use labels and references (`\label`, `\ref`, or the word processor's cross-reference feature).
- Refer to sections by number: "as shown in Section 4.3", not "as discussed above".
- When a concept returns, refer back to its definition instead of redefining it.

## 9. Consistency check

| Check | Method |
|---|---|
| Terms | Search the chapter for each "Avoid" variant on the sheet |
| Abbreviations | Each defined at first use; list of abbreviations matches |
| Notation | Same symbol means the same thing in every chapter |
| Numbering | Figures, tables, equations numbered by chapter |
| Captions | Same style; source credited for reproduced material |
| References | One citation style; all cited works listed |
| Claims about other chapters | Each matches what that chapter says |
| Research questions | Stated identically in the introduction, methodology, and conclusion |

## 10. Writing schedule advice

Write the methodology and results chapters while the work is fresh; write the introduction and conclusion last, after the argument is settled; revise the literature review near the end to add recent work.
