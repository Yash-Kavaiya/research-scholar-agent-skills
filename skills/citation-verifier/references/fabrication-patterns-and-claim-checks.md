# Fabricated-reference patterns and claim checks

## 1. Patterns seen in fabricated or corrupted references

| Pattern | What it looks like | How to detect |
|---|---|---|
| Plausible invention | Real-sounding title, real journal, real-looking authors; no record anywhere | Exact title search returns nothing in Crossref, OpenAlex, publisher site, or a general web search |
| Chimera | Real title with the wrong authors, year, or venue | Title matches a record whose authors differ |
| Borrowed DOI | A real DOI attached to an unrelated paper | DOI resolves to a different title |
| Malformed DOI | DOI with the right prefix but a non-existent suffix | DOI does not resolve |
| Real author, invented paper | Well-known author's name on a paper they never wrote | Absent from the author's publication list and ORCID |
| Wrong year or volume | Real paper, details off | Record found; fields disagree |
| Conference and journal versions mixed | Title of one, venue of the other | Two records exist; choose one and cite it consistently |
| Preprint cited as published | arXiv paper given a journal | Check whether a published version exists |

AI-generated references often combine a plausible title, a real venue in the right field, and authors who work in that area. A reference that "sounds right" is not evidence.

## 2. Investigating a not-found entry

1. Search the exact title in quotes in a general web search and on the publisher's or journal's site.
2. Search Google Scholar or Semantic Scholar by title and by first author.
3. Check the first author's publication list (institutional page, ORCID, DBLP for computing).
4. For books: library catalogues (WorldCat), the publisher, ISBN search.
5. For theses: the university repository, ProQuest, national thesis portals (for example Shodhganga in India).
6. For reports and standards: the issuing body's site.
7. If nothing is found anywhere, treat it as fabricated: remove it, and either find a real source for the claim or remove the claim.

Record each step taken so the conclusion can be explained.

## 3. Fixing a mismatch

Take every field from the authoritative record (publisher, Crossref, DBLP), not from the original entry. If the title matched but authors or venue differ, the original entry was corrupted: replace it entirely.

## 4. Checking claims against sources

| Verdict | Definition | Typical fix |
|---|---|---|
| Supports | The source states the claim, in comparable scope | None |
| Partially supports | Weaker, narrower, conditional, or on a different population | Add the condition or soften the wording |
| Does not support | Says something different, or the opposite | Remove the citation; find a correct source or delete the claim |
| Not the primary source | The source reports someone else's finding | Cite the original study |
| Cannot verify | No access to the relevant text | Obtain full text, or flag to the author |

## 5. Common claim distortions

| Distortion | Example |
|---|---|
| Scope creep | Source studies one population; text generalises to all |
| Strength inflation | Source "suggests"; text says "shows" or "proves" |
| Number drift | Source 23%; text "about a third" |
| Direction error | Source finds no effect; text cites it for an effect |
| Out-of-date | Source superseded or retracted |
| Citation chaining | Text cites a review that cites a study that says something different |
| Wrong year or edition | A different edition with different content |

## 6. Checking quotations and numbers

- Quotations: word for word, with page number; ellipses only where text was omitted and meaning kept.
- Numbers: digit for digit, with the table or page; check units and denominators.

## 7. Retractions and corrections

Check key references on the publisher's page for retraction or correction notices, and in Retraction Watch's database. A retracted paper may be cited only to discuss the retraction.

## 8. Reporting

For every reference: status and what was done. For every checked claim: verdict, evidence location, and the fix. List what could not be verified, so the author can complete the check.
