# Attribution, paraphrase, and text reuse

## 1. Forms of misattribution

| Form | Description | Detection |
|---|---|---|
| Verbatim copying | Source wording without quotation marks | Long shared word runs |
| Patchwriting | Source sentences with synonyms swapped and phrases rearranged | High trigram overlap; same sentence skeleton |
| Mosaic | Fragments of several sources stitched together | Several medium matches in one paragraph |
| Structural paraphrase | Same sequence of ideas and paragraph structure, new words | Side-by-side reading; script may miss it |
| Idea without credit | A specific finding or argument with no citation | Reading; script cannot detect |
| Translation without credit | Text translated from another language | Not detected lexically |
| Citation without quotation | Copied text with a citation but no quotation marks | Long run with a citation nearby |
| Self-plagiarism or text recycling | Reuse of the author's published text without citation or permission | Compare with the author's papers |
| Data or figure reuse | Figures, tables, or data from another work without credit or permission | Reading; reverse image search |

## 2. Reading the script's output

| Flag | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| verbatim | A run of 8 or more shared words outside quotation marks | Quote with citation and page, or rewrite from understanding |
| close_paraphrase | Half or more of the sentence's word trigrams appear in a source | Rewrite from understanding and cite |
| quoted | Shared run inside quotation marks | Check citation and page number |

Legitimate matches to dismiss with a note: technical terms, names of methods, datasets, instruments, standard phrases ("the results are shown in Table 2"), and short common phrases.

## 3. Paraphrasing from understanding

1. Read the passage until you can explain it without looking.
2. Close the source.
3. Write the point for your purpose: why it matters to your argument, how it relates to other sources.
4. Reopen the source and check accuracy, and that no distinctive phrase was carried over.
5. Cite the source.

| Source | Patchwriting (too close) | Paraphrase |
|---|---|---|
| "Deep neural networks have achieved remarkable success in image recognition tasks over the past decade." | "Deep neural networks have attained notable success in image classification tasks during the last ten years." | "Image classification accuracy improved sharply after 2012, driven largely by deep networks (Source, Year)." |

The paraphrase changes the structure, adds the writer's framing, and keeps the citation.

## 4. When to quote

- The exact wording is the point (a definition, a claim being analysed, a distinctive phrase).
- Legal, policy, or historical text.
- Paraphrase would distort.

Keep quotations short, with quotation marks, citation, and page number. Discipline norms differ: quotation is rare in sciences and engineering and more common in humanities.

## 5. Synthesis as prevention

Writing from a synthesis matrix (several sources per point) produces sentences that no single source contains, which avoids close paraphrase naturally.

## 6. Reuse of the author's own work

| Situation | Usual expectation |
|---|---|
| Methods text reused across the author's papers | Often tolerated in limited amounts; cite the earlier paper; follow the venue's policy |
| Introduction or results reused | Rewrite and cite |
| Conference paper extended to a journal | Allowed by many venues with a substantial extension (often a stated percentage of new material) and disclosure |
| Published papers as thesis chapters | Allowed under university rules with a declaration and publisher permission |
| Same paper submitted twice | Not allowed |

Check the publisher's copyright or licence terms for reusing published text.

## 7. Similarity reports from institutional software

- A similarity percentage counts matches, including properly quoted and cited text, references, and common phrases. It is not a plagiarism verdict.
- Institutions often exclude quotes and the bibliography when interpreting scores.
- Thresholds vary; a reviewer reads the matches, not only the number.
- This skill's check does not reproduce those scores, because it compares only with supplied sources.

## 8. Fix decision

| Passage | Fix |
|---|---|
| Long verbatim run, wording matters | Quote and cite with page |
| Long verbatim run, wording does not matter | Rewrite from understanding and cite |
| Close paraphrase | Rewrite from understanding and cite |
| Idea without citation | Add the citation |
| Not needed for the argument | Delete |
