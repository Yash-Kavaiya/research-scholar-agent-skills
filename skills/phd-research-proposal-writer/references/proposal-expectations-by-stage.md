# PhD proposal expectations by stage

## 1. What committees assess at each stage

| Stage | Typical length | What they assess |
|---|---|---|
| Admission | 1,500 to 3,000 words | Ability to identify a real problem; awareness of the literature; a plausible plan; fit with the department and a supervisor |
| Registration or synopsis (for example a research or departmental committee) | 3,000 to 10,000 words or more | A defined problem, a literature review, specific objectives, a detailed methodology, a timeline, and feasibility |
| Confirmation, upgrade, or qualifying review (after year one) | Report plus presentation | Progress, a refined plan, preliminary results, and readiness to continue |

The university's guidelines override these ranges.

## 2. Section guidance

| Section | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Title | Specific, under about 15 words, names the problem and approach | Grand claims; vague words ("study of", "aspects of") |
| Background | Context leading quickly to the problem | A history of the field |
| Literature review | Key works and the specific gap, organised by theme | A list of summaries |
| Problem statement | One paragraph that a committee can test against the review | A topic description |
| Aim and objectives | One aim; three or four objectives with measurable verbs, each a potential chapter | Objectives that are activities ("to read", "to study") |
| Research questions or hypotheses | Linked one-to-one with objectives | Questions the design cannot answer |
| Methodology | Design, data or materials, methods, analysis, validation, per objective; access confirmed | "Appropriate methods will be used" |
| Scope and limitations | What is in and out, and why | No boundaries |
| Timeline | Semester or quarter plan with writing and buffer time | A plan that ends exactly at the deadline |
| Expected contributions | Specific additions to knowledge, method, or practice | "Will revolutionise" |
| Ethics | Approvals needed and when | Ignoring human or animal data |
| References | Verified, consistent style | Unverified or padded lists |

## 3. Narrowing a topic

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Area | Natural language processing for health |
| Topic | Clinical text de-identification |
| Problem | De-identification tools trained on English notes perform poorly on code-mixed Hindi-English notes |
| Question | Can a model adapted with synthetic code-mixed notes reach the recall of English-trained tools on real code-mixed notes? |

A proposal needs at least the problem level.

## 4. Objectives that become chapters

| Objective | Likely chapter |
|---|---|
| To characterise <phenomenon> in <setting> | Exploratory or descriptive study |
| To develop <method> for <problem> | Method chapter |
| To evaluate <method> against <baselines> on <data> | Evaluation chapter |
| To examine how <users> adopt <tool> | Qualitative or mixed-methods study |

## 5. Timeline template (three to four years)

| Period | Typical work |
|---|---|
| Semester 1–2 | Coursework, literature review, refined proposal, ethics applications |
| Semester 2–4 | Objective 1 study; first paper |
| Semester 4–6 | Objective 2; second paper; confirmation or progress review |
| Semester 6–7 | Objective 3; third paper |
| Semester 7–8 | Thesis writing, submission, defence preparation |

Add buffers for ethics approval, data access, and supervisor review rounds.

## 6. Fit with a supervisor

Refer to the supervisor's recent work specifically: the method or dataset you would build on, and how your question extends it. Verify the papers, and keep it to two or three sentences.

## 7. Feasibility evidence

| Risk | Evidence that addresses it |
|---|---|
| Data access | Named dataset with licence; letter from a data holder |
| Participants | Recruitment route; size of the population |
| Skills | Coursework, prior projects, training plan |
| Equipment or compute | Department facilities; cloud allocation |
| Time | Timeline with buffer |

## 8. Common weaknesses noted by committees

Too broad; gap asserted without evidence; objectives that do not match the questions; methodology too vague to evaluate; unrealistic timeline; references unverified or outdated; ethics left unaddressed where people are involved.
