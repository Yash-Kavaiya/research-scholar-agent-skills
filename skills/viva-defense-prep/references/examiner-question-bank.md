# Examiner question bank and answering techniques

## 1. Questions by category

### Opening
- Summarise your thesis in three to five minutes.
- What is your main contribution? If you had one sentence, what would it be?
- Why did you choose this topic?
- What are you most proud of in this work?

### Contribution and originality
- What do we know now that we did not know before your thesis?
- How does your work differ from <closest work>?
- Which part of the thesis is most original?
- What would have happened to the field if you had not done this?

### Literature and positioning
- Which authors or papers influenced you most, and why?
- What has been published since you submitted that bears on your work?
- Why did you not engage with <school, theory, or method>?
- Where does your work sit relative to <competing approach>?

### Theory and concepts
- Define <core concept> in your own words.
- Why this theoretical framework rather than <alternative>?
- How does your work extend or challenge the theory?

### Methodology
- Why this design and not <alternative>?
- How did you choose your sample, data, or parameters?
- What are the main threats to validity, and how did you address them?
- If you started again, what would you change in the method?
- How did you ensure reliability or trustworthiness?

### Results
- Walk me through Figure <n> / Table <n>.
- How confident are you in <result>? How big is the effect, and does it matter?
- What surprised you?
- How do you explain <anomalous or negative result>?
- Is this result robust to <change>?

### Limitations
- What is the weakest part of the thesis?
- How far do your findings generalise?
- What would a critic of your approach say?

### Implications and future work
- Who should care about these findings, and what should they do differently?
- What is the next study you would run?
- How would you get funding for it?

### Ownership and process
- In the co-authored chapters, which parts were your work?
- How did you implement <component>?
- What did you learn about doing research?

### Fundamentals
- Derive or explain <basic result the method relies on>.
- What assumptions does <statistical test or algorithm> make?

## 2. Answer structure

1. Direct answer in one sentence.
2. Reasoning or evidence (chapter and page in your head).
3. Acknowledge the limit or alternative view.
4. Stop.

Aim for one to two minutes unless the examiner asks for more.

## 3. Hard-question techniques

| Situation | Technique |
|---|---|
| A real weakness | Agree it is a limitation; explain why the choice was made; state its effect on conclusions; say what would address it |
| A question you do not understand | Ask for clarification or restate it: "Do you mean ...?" |
| A question you cannot answer | Say so; reason aloud about how you would find out |
| The examiner is right and you are wrong | Concede; note it as a correction |
| A hostile or leading question | Stay calm; answer the substance; keep your evidence central |
| A question outside the thesis | Connect to what you know; be clear about the boundary |
| A long multi-part question | Note the parts; answer in order |

## 4. Examiner-specific preparation

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| Identify interests | Read the examiners' recent papers and their methods |
| Map overlaps | Where their work touches your topic, method, or theory |
| Predict questions | From their perspective (a methodologist asks about design; a theorist about framework) |
| Cite them fairly | If relevant, know how your work relates to theirs |

## 5. Mock session protocol

| Round | Style | Feedback focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Friendly, standard questions | Content accuracy and completeness |
| 2 | Probing follow-ups | Structure and length of answers |
| 3 | Hard and hostile questions | Composure, conceding, reasoning aloud |
| 4 | Mixed, timed, full-length | Endurance, consistency |

Record recurring problems: rambling, defensiveness, vague evidence, forgetting chapter content.

## 6. Final-week preparation

- Reread the whole thesis; tab key pages (method choices, main results, limitations).
- Prepare a corrections list (typos, errors found) to hand over if appropriate.
- Check new publications in the area since submission.
- Rehearse the three-minute summary aloud daily.
- Prepare the presentation if required, and test the equipment.
- Plan logistics: time, place, documents, water.

## 7. Outcomes (general; check regulations)

Common outcomes include pass without corrections, pass with minor corrections, pass with major corrections or revision and resubmission, a lower award, or fail. Regulations define timelines for corrections.
