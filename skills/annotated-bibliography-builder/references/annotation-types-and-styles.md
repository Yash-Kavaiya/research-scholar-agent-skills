# Annotation types, evaluation criteria, and citation formats

## 1. Types of annotation

| Type | Contains | Typical length | Asked for when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive (indicative) | What the source covers | 50 to 100 words | A reading list |
| Informative (summary) | The argument, method, and findings | 100 to 150 words | Coursework showing comprehension |
| Evaluative (critical) | Summary plus assessment of quality and limits | 150 to 200 words | Most university assignments |
| Reflective | Summary, evaluation, and use in the project | 150 to 250 words | Proposals and thesis preparation |

Confirm which type the assignment requires. If unstated, write all three parts.

## 2. The three parts

**Summary.** The question or purpose, the approach or method, and the main finding or argument. Two to four sentences, in your own words.

**Evaluation.** Choose the criteria that apply:

| Criterion | Questions |
|---|---|
| Authority | Who wrote it; their expertise and affiliation; the venue's standing; peer review |
| Method | Design, sample size, data quality, appropriateness for the claim |
| Currency | Publication date relative to the field's pace; superseded or still standard |
| Objectivity | Funding, stated position, balance of evidence |
| Coverage | Scope; what it leaves out |
| Comparison | How it agrees or disagrees with other sources in the list |

**Relevance.** What the source does for this project. Use one of these roles and say which:

| Role | Example wording |
|---|---|
| Background | "Provides the definition of X adopted in this study." |
| Evidence | "Supports the claim that ..." |
| Method | "The sampling approach will be adapted for ..." |
| Counter-position | "Offers the main objection to ..., which the discussion must address." |
| Gap | "Shows that ... has not been examined in ..." |

## 3. Citation formats for common source types

Check the current edition of the manual for anything unusual. These are the common patterns.

### APA 7th

- Journal article: Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article in sentence case. *Journal Title in Title Case, volume*(issue), pages. https://doi.org/xxxxx
- Book: Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of book in sentence case* (edition). Publisher.
- Chapter: Author, A. A. (Year). Title of chapter. In E. E. Editor (Ed.), *Title of book* (pp. xx–xx). Publisher.
- Conference paper in proceedings: Author, A. A. (Year). Title of paper. In *Proceedings title* (pp. xx–xx). Publisher. DOI
- Up to 20 authors are listed; in text, three or more authors become "First et al."

### MLA 9th

- Journal article: Author Surname, First Name, and First Name Surname. "Title of Article in Title Case." *Journal Title*, vol. X, no. X, Year, pp. xx–xx. DOI.
- Book: Surname, First Name. *Title of Book*. Publisher, Year.

### Chicago author-date

- Journal article: Surname, First Name, and First Name Surname. Year. "Title of Article." *Journal Title* volume (issue): pages. DOI.

### IEEE

- Journal article: A. A. Author and B. B. Author, "Title of article in sentence case," *Abbrev. Journal Title*, vol. X, no. X, pp. xx–xx, Month Year, doi: xxxxx.
- Conference paper: A. A. Author, "Title of paper," in *Proc. Abbrev. Conf. Name*, City, Country, Year, pp. xx–xx.

### Harvard (varies by institution)

- Journal article: Surname, Initial. and Surname, Initial. (Year) 'Title of article', *Journal Title*, volume(issue), pp. xx–xx. doi: xxxxx.

Generate citations from verified metadata with a citation processor where possible (see citation-style-converter) instead of typing them.

## 4. Layout rules

| Style | Layout |
|---|---|
| APA | Hanging-indent reference; annotation as a new paragraph, indented 0.5 in from the margin; double-spaced; alphabetical by first author |
| MLA | Hanging-indent reference; annotation indented 1 in from the margin; alphabetical |
| Chicago | Hanging-indent reference; annotation on a new line, indented |
| Thematic lists | Headings per theme, alphabetical within each |

## 5. Voice and tense

- Third person. No "I think" unless a reflective annotation is requested.
- Present tense for what the source argues ("The authors argue"); past for what a study did ("The study surveyed").
- Do not begin every annotation the same way. Vary: "This randomised trial ...", "Drawing on interviews with ...", "The authors challenge ...".
- Do not copy the abstract. An annotation written from the abstract alone is detectable and adds nothing.

## 6. Example (evaluative and reflective, APA)

Garcia, M., & Chen, L. (2022). Audio versus written feedback in large online courses. *Journal of Learning Design, 15*(2), 101–118.

> This randomised study compared audio and written instructor feedback for 240 undergraduates over one semester and found higher reported satisfaction and more feedback uptake in the audio group. The design is strong for its question, but the study took place in a single course at one institution, and uptake was self-reported. Compared with the survey evidence elsewhere in this list, it offers the only experimental test of modality. It supports this project's assumption that feedback format affects engagement and supplies a measure of uptake that can be adapted; it does not address peer feedback.

(The reference above is an invented illustration of format, not a real source.)

## 7. Checks

- Metadata verified against the publisher or Crossref.
- One style throughout; correct capitalisation and italics for that style.
- Word count within the limit.
- Each annotation distinguishes what the source says from the annotator's assessment.
- Abstract-only entries marked.
