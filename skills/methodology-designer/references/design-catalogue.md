# Research design catalogue

## 1. Quantitative designs

| Design | Answers | Strength | Main threats | Typical analysis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Randomised controlled experiment | Does X cause Y? | Strongest causal inference | Attrition, contamination, limited external validity | t test, ANOVA, ANCOVA, mixed models |
| Quasi-experiment (non-equivalent groups) | Does X cause Y, where randomisation is impossible? | Feasible in real settings | Selection bias, baseline differences | ANCOVA, difference-in-differences, propensity scores |
| Interrupted time series | Did a change at a known time alter a trend? | Uses routine records | Co-occurring events, seasonality | Segmented regression |
| Within-subject (repeated measures, crossover) | Does each person respond differently under conditions? | High power with few participants | Order and carry-over effects | Paired tests, repeated-measures or mixed models |
| Cross-sectional survey | How common is X? Is X associated with Y? | Fast and cheap | No temporal order, common-method bias, non-response | Descriptives, correlation, regression |
| Cohort (longitudinal) | Does exposure precede outcome? | Temporal order established | Attrition, confounding, time | Survival analysis, mixed models |
| Case-control | What exposures distinguish cases? | Efficient for rare outcomes | Recall and selection bias | Odds ratios, logistic regression |
| Secondary data analysis | Any of the above with existing records | Large samples, no collection cost | Variables not designed for the question | As appropriate |
| Simulation or benchmark study | How does a method behave under controlled conditions? | Full control, repeatable | Realism of the setup | Comparison across conditions with repeated runs |

## 2. Qualitative designs

| Design | Answers | Data | Sample | Analysis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phenomenology | What is the lived experience of X? | In-depth interviews | Small, people who have lived it | Interpretative phenomenological analysis |
| Grounded theory | What process or theory explains X? | Interviews, observation, iteratively | Theoretical sampling to saturation | Open, axial, and selective coding; constant comparison |
| Case study | How and why does X work in this bounded case? | Multiple sources per case | One or a few cases, purposively chosen | Within-case and cross-case analysis |
| Ethnography | How does a group live and make meaning? | Prolonged observation, field notes | One setting | Thick description, thematic analysis |
| Narrative inquiry | How do people story their experience? | Life-history interviews, documents | Few participants | Narrative analysis |
| Qualitative descriptive | What do people say about X? | Interviews, focus groups | Purposive, moderate | Thematic or content analysis |

## 3. Mixed-methods designs

| Design | Sequence | Use when | Integration point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Convergent | Quantitative and qualitative together | Two kinds of evidence on one question | Compare and merge results in interpretation (joint display) |
| Explanatory sequential | Quantitative, then qualitative | Numbers need explaining | Quantitative results select participants or topics for phase two |
| Exploratory sequential | Qualitative, then quantitative | Constructs or instruments must be developed first | Qualitative findings build the instrument or hypotheses |
| Embedded | One strand inside the other | A trial with a process evaluation | Secondary strand informs the primary |

State why both strands are needed and where they are combined. Two studies reported side by side are not mixed methods.

## 4. Other families

| Family | Use | Core steps |
|---|---|---|
| Design science | Building and evaluating an artefact | Problem, objectives, design, demonstration, evaluation, communication |
| Action research | Improving practice with participants | Plan, act, observe, reflect, in cycles |
| Delphi study | Expert consensus | Rounds of anonymous rating with feedback |
| Systematic or scoping review | Synthesising existing studies | See systematic-review-prisma |

## 5. Sampling

| Method | Description | Use | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple random | Every unit has equal chance | A complete sampling frame exists | Needs the frame |
| Stratified | Random within strata | Subgroups must be represented | Needs stratum information |
| Cluster | Random groups, then all or some within | Units come in natural groups | Larger sample needed (design effect) |
| Systematic | Every k-th unit | Ordered lists | Hidden periodicity |
| Convenience | Whoever is available | Pilots, constrained projects | Weak generalisation; say so |
| Purposive | Chosen for relevance | Qualitative work | Depends on the researcher's judgement |
| Snowball | Participants refer others | Hard-to-reach groups | Network bias |
| Theoretical | Chosen to develop emerging theory | Grounded theory | Cannot be fully planned in advance |
| Quota | Fixed numbers per category, non-random | Surveys without a frame | Not probability-based |

## 6. Validity threats and remedies (quantitative)

| Threat | What goes wrong | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Selection | Groups differ before treatment | Randomise; match; adjust for baseline |
| History | An outside event affects the outcome | Control group measured at the same time |
| Maturation | Participants change naturally | Control group |
| Testing | Taking the pretest changes the post-test | Control group; alternate forms |
| Instrumentation | The measure changes between occasions | Same instrument, trained raters |
| Regression to the mean | Extreme scorers drift toward average | Control group; avoid selecting on extremes |
| Attrition | Dropout differs by group | Track and report; intention-to-treat; sensitivity analysis |
| Diffusion | Control group receives the treatment | Separate groups or cluster-randomise |
| Experimenter and demand effects | Expectations shape behaviour | Blinding, scripts, neutral instructions |
| Low statistical power | Real effects missed | Power analysis |
| Construct invalidity | The measure does not capture the concept | Validated instruments, multiple measures |
| Limited external validity | Findings do not travel | Diverse sample; replication; state the scope |

## 7. Trustworthiness (qualitative)

| Criterion | Techniques |
|---|---|
| Credibility | Prolonged engagement, triangulation, member checking, peer debriefing, negative case analysis |
| Transferability | Thick description of context and participants |
| Dependability | Audit trail, documented procedures, code-recode checks |
| Confirmability | Reflexive journal, audit trail, quotes that support each theme |

## 8. Sample size bases

| Approach | Basis |
|---|---|
| Quantitative, confirmatory | Power analysis for the primary outcome |
| Survey estimating a proportion | Margin of error and confidence level |
| Factor analysis | Roughly 5 to 10 respondents per item, with a floor of about 200 for stable solutions |
| Qualitative interviews | Saturation; plan a range (often 12 to 20 for a fairly homogeneous group) and a stopping rule |
| Case study | Number and choice of cases justified by replication logic |

## 9. Justifying a design in writing

For each choice give: what was chosen, why it fits the question, which alternative was considered, and why it was not used. Cite a methodological authority for the design after verifying the reference.
