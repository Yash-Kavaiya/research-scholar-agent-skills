# Research ethics: principles, review routes, risk, and data protection

## 1. Principles that committees apply

| Principle | Source tradition | What committees look for |
|---|---|---|
| Respect for persons and autonomy | Belmont Report; Declaration of Helsinki | Informed, voluntary consent; extra protection for those with reduced autonomy |
| Beneficence and non-maleficence | Belmont; Helsinki | Risks minimised and justified by benefits |
| Justice | Belmont | Fair selection of participants; no exploitation of convenient vulnerable groups |
| Privacy and confidentiality | Data protection law; professional codes | Minimal data, secure handling, clear limits of confidentiality |
| Integrity | Research integrity codes | Honest design, reporting, and disclosure of conflicts |

## 2. Frameworks by jurisdiction (confirm locally)

| Jurisdiction | Human-participant rules | Data protection |
|---|---|---|
| United States | Common Rule (45 CFR 46); FDA rules for clinical investigations | HIPAA for health data; state laws |
| India | ICMR National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants; institutional ethics committees registered as required | Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 |
| European Union | National implementations; Clinical Trials Regulation for trials | GDPR |
| United Kingdom | UK Policy Framework for Health and Social Care Research; university processes | UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018 |
| Canada | TCPS 2 | PIPEDA and provincial laws |
| Australia | National Statement on Ethical Conduct in Human Research | Privacy Act 1988 |

The institution's research office decides which rules apply and which committee reviews the study.

## 3. Review routes

| Route | Usually covers | Decided by |
|---|---|---|
| Exempt or not human-participant research | Analysis of public, non-identifiable data; some educational and survey research with no identifiers and minimal risk | The committee or its delegate, not the researcher |
| Expedited or delegated | Minimal-risk research such as anonymous surveys or interviews on non-sensitive topics | Committee reviewer |
| Full board | Greater than minimal risk, vulnerable groups, deception, sensitive topics, interventions | Convened committee |

Minimal risk: the probability and size of harm are no greater than those met in daily life or routine examinations.

## 4. Vulnerable participants and added safeguards

| Group | Safeguards |
|---|---|
| Children | Parental permission plus the child's assent; age-appropriate materials |
| People with impaired decision-making capacity | Capacity assessment; legally authorised representative; ongoing assent |
| Students or employees of the researcher | Recruitment by a third party; anonymity from the researcher; no effect on grades or employment |
| Patients | Separation of care from research; clinician not as recruiter where possible |
| Prisoners and others in institutions | Voluntariness safeguards; committee with relevant expertise |
| Economically or socially disadvantaged groups | Proportionate incentives; no undue inducement |
| Pregnant participants | Assessment of foetal risk where relevant |

## 5. Risk assessment

| Risk type | Examples | Mitigations |
|---|---|---|
| Physical | Exercise, procedures, substances | Screening, supervision, stopping rules |
| Psychological | Distress recalling trauma, stress tasks | Warning in the information sheet, right to skip, distress protocol, referral list |
| Social and reputational | Disclosure of views, stigma | Anonymity, careful reporting, aggregated results |
| Legal | Disclosure of illegal activity | Clear limits of confidentiality; avoid collecting such details |
| Economic | Time costs, job consequences | Fair compensation; separation from employer |
| Privacy | Re-identification, data breach | Minimisation, pseudonymisation, encryption, access control |
| Researcher safety | Lone fieldwork, home visits | Check-in procedures, risk assessment for staff |

Rate each risk's likelihood and severity, and describe how the mitigation reduces it.

## 6. Informed consent elements

Purpose; what participation involves and its duration; risks and discomforts; benefits (or none); voluntariness and withdrawal without penalty, including the point after which data cannot be withdrawn; confidentiality and its limits; data storage, retention, and sharing; compensation; contact for questions and complaints; approval reference; consent for optional elements (recording, future contact, data sharing) recorded separately.

Plain language: short sentences, everyday words, second person, headings as questions ("What will happen if I take part?").

## 7. Consent modes

| Mode | When appropriate |
|---|---|
| Written signed | Default for most studies |
| Online click-through | Anonymous online surveys |
| Verbal, recorded or documented | Low literacy, phone interviews, signatures that would identify participants |
| Waiver or alteration | Minimal risk, impracticable without it, rights not harmed; decided by the committee |
| Deferred | Emergency research under specific rules |

## 8. Identifiability terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Identifiable | Direct identifiers present |
| Pseudonymised (coded) | Identifiers replaced by codes; a key exists. Still personal data under GDPR |
| De-identified | Identifiers removed; re-identification risk reduced but not necessarily zero |
| Anonymous | No one, including the researcher, can link data to a person. Must be true of the collection method, not only the dataset |

## 9. Data management plan contents

Data types and formats; volume; collection methods; identifiers and how they are separated; storage location and encryption; backup; access control; transfer methods; retention period and secure deletion; sharing and repository, with consent for it; legal basis for processing (GDPR) or consent (DPDP Act); international transfers; responsibilities.

## 10. Special topics

| Topic | Points to address |
|---|---|
| Deception | Why necessary; no alternative; harmless; debriefing |
| Online and social media data | Platform terms; expectation of privacy; quoting that can be searched back to a person |
| AI tools processing participant data | Whether consent and the data plan cover it; where data is sent; retention by the provider |
| Incentives | Proportionate, not coercive; payment schedule if participants withdraw |
| Incidental findings | What will be disclosed and how |
| Indigenous and community research | Community consent and governance, data sovereignty |
