# Statement craft

## 1. Interview questions to collect material

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| Origin of interest | What problem first caught your attention? What did you do about it? |
| Each project | What was the question? What did you do yourself? What went wrong and how did you fix it? What was the result? What did you learn that you would not have learned otherwise? |
| Skills | Which methods and tools can you use independently? Evidence? |
| Research interests | Which two or three problems would you work on now? Why are they open? Which papers shaped your view? |
| Programme fit | Which faculty, labs, courses, or resources matter to you, and why? |
| Goals | What do you want to be doing in five to ten years? |
| Context | Anything in the record that needs a brief explanation? |

## 2. Showing, not asserting

| Assertion | Showing |
|---|---|
| "I am passionate about machine learning." | "I spent my final-year project making a crop-disease classifier work on phone photos taken in poor light; accuracy rose from 61% to 84% after I built a synthetic-noise augmentation." |
| "I have strong research skills." | "I designed the survey, collected 312 responses, and ran the regression analysis reported in our workshop paper." |
| "I am a hard worker." | (omit; let the work show it) |

## 3. SOP structure (master's or PhD)

| Paragraph | Content | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Research interest stated directly, with the experience or question behind it | 10–15% |
| 2–3 | Two or three experiences in depth: problem, contribution, result, lesson | 35–45% |
| 4 | Research interests and questions for graduate study, showing reading | 15–20% |
| 5 | Fit: two or three faculty with specific links; resources | 15–20% |
| 6 | Goals and close | 5–10% |

## 4. Research statement structure (postdoc or faculty)

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Vision | One paragraph on the research programme |
| Past and current work | Two or three threads: problem, contribution, results, impact, with citations to the candidate's papers |
| Future agenda | Two or three directions for 3 to 5 years; first projects; methods; funding sources; student involvement |
| Fit | Collaborations, facilities, and how the agenda complements the department |

Distinguish the candidate's agenda from the advisor's.

## 5. Personal statement versus SOP

| Document | Focus |
|---|---|
| Statement of purpose | Academic and research preparation, interests, fit |
| Personal statement or diversity statement | Background, experiences, perspectives, and contributions to the community, as the prompt asks |

Answer the prompt that was given; some programmes use different names for the same document.

## 6. Tailoring

| Element | Changes per programme |
|---|---|
| Faculty and labs | Yes; verified on the department site |
| Resources and courses | Yes |
| Research interests emphasis | Possibly, to match the programme's strengths |
| Experiences | Usually the same |
| Programme name | Yes; check every occurrence |

## 7. Explaining a weakness

Two or three sentences: what happened, what changed, and evidence of the change (later grades, work). No excuses; no long narrative.

## 8. Cutting to the limit

1. Remove generic sentences that could appear in anyone's statement.
2. Shorten the opening.
3. Merge minor experiences into one sentence.
4. Keep specific details, numbers, and outcomes.

## 9. Openings to avoid

Quotations; childhood stories unrelated to research; dictionary definitions; "Since I was young"; praise of the university's ranking.

## 10. Final checks

Prompt answered; limit met; programme and faculty names correct; every claim true and supportable in an interview; read aloud for flow; policy on AI assistance checked.
