# Item writing, response scales, and validation

## 1. Item-writing rules with examples

| Rule | Faulty item | Revised |
|---|---|---|
| One idea per item | The library is quiet and well lit. | The library is quiet. / The library is well lit. |
| No leading wording | How useful did you find our excellent tutoring service? | How useful was the tutoring service? |
| No loaded terms | Do you support the wasteful new parking fee? | Do you support the new parking fee? |
| No double negatives | I do not disagree that exams are not fair. | Exams are fair. |
| Specific time frame | Do you exercise regularly? | In the past 7 days, on how many days did you exercise for at least 30 minutes? |
| Plain words | To what extent do you utilise asynchronous modalities? | How often do you use recorded lectures? |
| Answerable from memory | How many emails did you send last year? | About how many emails did you send yesterday? |
| No assumptions | How much do you enjoy your commute by bus? | How do you usually travel to campus? (then follow-up) |
| Neutral social desirability | Do you cheat on exams? | Indirect or anonymous formats; normalise the behaviour ("Some students ...") |
| Exhaustive, exclusive options | Age: 18–25, 25–35, 35+ | 18–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45 or older, Prefer not to say |

## 2. Response formats

| Format | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement (Likert) | Attitudes and beliefs stated as statements | 5 or 7 points, all labelled, symmetric, neutral midpoint unless a forced choice is justified |
| Frequency | Behaviour | Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Always, or concrete counts |
| Quality or satisfaction | Evaluations | Very poor to Very good; Very dissatisfied to Very satisfied |
| Semantic differential | Perception of an object | Bipolar adjective pairs (difficult ... easy) |
| Numeric rating (0–10) | Intensity, likelihood | Label the ends |
| Ranking | Priorities among few options | Hard on mobile; limit to about five options |
| Multiple choice, single answer | Categories | Add Other (please specify) when the list may be incomplete |
| Check all that apply | Multiple categories | Weaker data than a yes/no for each option |
| Open text | Reasons, examples | Few; place after related closed items |

Keep one format and direction within a block. Labelled points reduce interpretation differences between respondents.

## 3. Questionnaire order

1. Title, purpose, duration, consent.
2. Screening and eligibility.
3. Easy, interesting, non-threatening items.
4. Core constructs, grouped by topic, general before specific.
5. Sensitive items.
6. Demographics, only those needed.
7. Optional open comment and thanks.

Use skip logic so respondents see only relevant items. For online surveys, a progress bar and mobile-friendly layout reduce dropout.

## 4. Data-quality controls

| Control | Example | Report in methods |
|---|---|---|
| Attention check | "Please select Disagree for this item." | Number excluded and the rule |
| Completion time floor | Exclude responses under one third of the median time | The threshold |
| Straight-lining check | Identical answers across a block including reverse items | The rule |
| Duplicate detection | IP or device checks where ethical and permitted | The method |

## 5. Validity evidence

| Type | Question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Do the items cover the construct? | Expert review; content validity index (item-level CVI of 0.78 or higher with 6 to 10 experts is a common criterion) |
| Face and response process | Do respondents read items as intended? | Cognitive interviews (think-aloud, probing) with 5 to 10 people |
| Structural | Do items group as the theory says? | Exploratory factor analysis in development; confirmatory factor analysis in a new sample (common fit guides: CFI and TLI above 0.95, RMSEA below 0.06, SRMR below 0.08) |
| Convergent | Does the scale correlate with related measures? | Correlations in the expected direction |
| Discriminant | Is it distinct from different constructs? | Low correlations; AVE comparisons |
| Criterion | Does it predict a relevant outcome? | Concurrent or predictive correlations |
| Measurement invariance | Does it work the same across groups? | Multi-group CFA |

## 6. Reliability

| Index | Use | Common guide |
|---|---|---|
| Cronbach's alpha | Internal consistency of a multi-item scale | 0.70 or higher for research; above 0.95 can signal redundant items |
| McDonald's omega | Internal consistency without equal loadings | Same guide; preferred by many methodologists |
| Corrected item-total correlation | Item contribution | Below about 0.30 flags an item |
| Test-retest (ICC) | Stability over time | 0.75 or higher is good |
| Inter-rater (kappa, ICC) | Coded or rated items | Kappa 0.61 to 0.80 is substantial |

## 7. Pilot plan

| Stage | Participants | Checks |
|---|---|---|
| Expert review | 3 to 10 subject experts | Relevance and coverage per item |
| Cognitive interviews | 5 to 10 from the target group | Comprehension, recall, judgement, response mapping |
| Field pilot | 20 to 50, or more for factor analysis | Completion time, missing data, item variance, floor and ceiling effects, reliability |

Revise after each stage and record the changes for the methods section.

## 8. Adapting and translating existing scales

- Obtain permission or confirm the licence; cite the original.
- Record every change (wording, number of points, items dropped) and call the scale adapted.
- Translation: forward translation by two people, reconciliation, back translation, expert committee review, cognitive testing in the target language.
- Recheck reliability and, where possible, factor structure in the new population.

## 9. Interview guides

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Opening | Purpose, consent, recording, a warm-up question about the participant's role |
| Main questions | 6 to 10 open questions in a logical order, each tied to a research question |
| Probes | "Can you give an example?", "What happened next?", "How did that affect you?" |
| Closing | "Is there anything else you would like to add?", thanks, next steps |

Avoid yes/no, leading, and double questions. Pilot the guide with one or two participants.

## 10. Analysis implications of design choices

- Single Likert items are ordinal; analyse with non-parametric or ordinal methods.
- Multi-item scale scores (sum or mean) are usually treated as continuous.
- Check-all-that-apply items need one binary variable per option.
- Open text needs coding (see qualitative-coding-assistant).
