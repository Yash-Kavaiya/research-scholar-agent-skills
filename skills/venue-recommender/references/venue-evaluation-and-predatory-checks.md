# Venue evaluation and predatory-venue checks

## 1. Finding venues where similar work appears

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| Tally venues of the closest papers | Search the topic for the last three years; count venues among relevant results |
| References of the draft | Venues of the most-cited references |
| Publisher matchers | Elsevier Journal Finder, Springer Nature Journal Suggester, Wiley Journal Finder, IEEE Publication Recommender, JANE (biomedical) |
| Conference rankings and lists | CORE (computing), field-specific lists |
| Senior colleagues and supervisors | Where the field's respected work appears |

Matchers suggest scope fit only; they do not evaluate quality or legitimacy.

## 2. Facts to collect, and the source for each

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| Aims and scope | Journal or conference website |
| Indexing in Scopus | Scopus source list (search by ISSN) |
| Indexing in Web of Science (SCIE, SSCI, AHCI, ESCI) | Clarivate Master Journal List |
| Open-access journal listing | DOAJ |
| Impact factor and quartile | Journal Citation Reports (subscription) |
| CiteScore | Scopus |
| SJR and quartile | SCImago Journal Rank |
| CS conference rank | CORE portal |
| UGC-CARE listing (India) | UGC-CARE list, where institutions require it |
| ABDC rank (business, Australia) | ABDC journal list |
| Article processing charge | Journal's author guidelines |
| Review and publication times | Journal pages; editorial reports; otherwise unknown |
| Acceptance rate | Journal or conference reports; often not published |
| Deadlines (conferences) | Current call for papers |
| Preprint policy | Journal policy page; Sherpa Romeo |

Record the date each fact was checked; metrics and listings change yearly.

## 3. Metrics and what they do not show

| Metric | Measures | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Journal impact factor | Average citations to recent items | Skewed by few highly cited papers; field-dependent |
| CiteScore | Similar, over a longer window, Scopus data | Same cautions |
| SJR | Citations weighted by the prestige of citing journals | Field-dependent |
| Quartile | Rank within a subject category | Category assignment matters |
| CORE rank | Expert-informed conference tier | Computing only; updated periodically |
| h5-index (Google Scholar) | Citation impact of recent output | Favours large venues |

Compare metrics only within a field.

## 4. Predatory and questionable venue signals

| Signal | Example |
|---|---|
| Unsolicited invitation emails with flattery | "Dear esteemed researcher, we invite your valuable manuscript" |
| Promised fast acceptance | "Peer review in 72 hours" |
| Fees hidden or revealed only after acceptance | No APC on the website |
| Scope covering unrelated fields | "Journal of Engineering, Medicine, and Social Sciences" |
| Unverifiable editorial board | Editors who do not list the role on their own pages |
| False or misleading indexing claims | "Indexed in Google Scholar" presented as quality; fake metrics such as "Global Impact Factor" |
| Name or website imitating a known journal | Hijacked or cloned journals with a similar URL |
| Poor-quality published papers | Obvious errors, off-topic content |
| No retraction or correction policy | |
| No ISSN, or an ISSN belonging to another title | Check in the ISSN portal |

Hijacked journals: always reach the journal's website from the indexing database's record or the publisher's site, not from an email link or a search result.

Use the Think. Check. Submit. checklist; check DOAJ membership for open-access journals and COPE membership as a positive signal.

## 5. Fit assessment

| Aspect | High fit | Low fit |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | The journal recently published similar work | The topic is absent from the last two years |
| Audience | The readers need this result | Readers would find it peripheral |
| Article type | The journal publishes this type and length | Type or length not accepted |
| Method standard | Similar rigour in published papers | Much higher or lower standard |

## 6. Building the ladder

| Rung | Choice |
|---|---|
| Ambitious | The best venue where the paper has a realistic chance |
| Realistic (two or three) | Good scope fit, matching standard |
| Safe | Solid, legitimate venue with high fit and faster review |

Plan the reformatting between rungs (templates, length limits, anonymity). Submit to one venue at a time.

## 7. Conference versus journal

| Factor | Conference | Journal |
|---|---|---|
| Timeline | Fixed deadlines, decisions in about 2 to 4 months | Rolling; first decision weeks to months |
| Length | Strict page limits | Usually longer |
| Revision | Rebuttal, then accept or reject | Revise-and-resubmit cycles |
| Field norms | Primary venue in much of CS | Primary venue in most other fields |
| Extension | Journal versions of conference papers often allowed with new material | Check prior-publication policy |
