# Plain-language writing guide

## 1. Audience levels

| Audience | Assumes | Explain | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| General public | No field knowledge | Every technical term; why the problem matters in daily terms | 600–1,000 words |
| Practitioners | Domain knowledge, not research methods | Methods briefly; focus on what to do with the result | 600–1,200 words |
| Researchers in neighbouring fields | Research methods, not this subfield | Subfield terms and why the result matters to them | 800–1,500 words |
| Students | Basic field knowledge | Concepts with examples | 800–1,500 words |
| Policymakers | No technical knowledge; time-poor | Implications, evidence strength, costs and benefits | 400–800 words or a one-page brief |

## 2. Jargon replacements (examples)

| Term | Plain alternative |
|---|---|
| statistically significant | unlikely to be due to chance alone (and then give the size) |
| randomised controlled trial | a study where participants were assigned to groups by chance |
| cohort study | a study that followed a group of people over time |
| model (ML) | a computer program trained on examples |
| dataset | collection of examples |
| parameters | the adjustable settings a model learns |
| benchmark | a standard test that researchers use to compare methods |
| outperforms | scores higher than |
| correlation | tends to occur together |
| confidence interval | the range in which the true value probably lies |

Explain a term once, then use it consistently.

## 3. Numbers

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Use natural frequencies | "3 in 100 people" rather than "3%" when risk is the point |
| Give absolute alongside relative | "halved the risk, from 2 in 1,000 to 1 in 1,000" |
| Round sensibly | "about 40%" rather than "39.7%" unless precision matters |
| Compare with something familiar | "about the time of a short coffee break" for 10 minutes of processing |
| State the denominator and the group | "among 180 adults with insomnia" |

## 4. Caveats to keep

| Study feature | How to say it |
|---|---|
| Preprint | "The study has not yet been peer reviewed." |
| Animal or cell study | "In mice" in the headline and first paragraph |
| Simulation or benchmark | "In computer tests on standard datasets" |
| Small sample | "In a small study of 24 people" |
| Correlational design | "linked to" or "associated with", never "causes" |
| Single setting | "at one hospital", "on English text" |
| Early-stage | "an early step; it is not yet ready for use by ..." |

## 5. Headlines

| Misleading | Accurate |
|---|---|
| "AI cures insomnia" | "An app-based programme reduced insomnia symptoms more than standard advice in a 12-week trial" |
| "Scientists prove coffee prevents cancer" | "Coffee drinking was linked to lower risk of one cancer in a large observational study" |
| "Breakthrough model beats humans" | "A model matched expert accuracy on a standard test of X" |

Test: would someone reading only the headline believe something the paper does not show?

## 6. Analogies

Choose an analogy that maps the mechanism, not just the vibe; say where it breaks down if readers might take it too far. One analogy per idea.

## 7. Platform conventions

| Platform | Conventions |
|---|---|
| Blog or lab website | Headings; one image; links to paper and code; 600–1,200 words |
| Thread (X, Bluesky, Mastodon) | First post stands alone with the finding and an image; one idea per post; final post with link and credits; check the platform's character limit |
| LinkedIn | First two lines visible before "see more"; 150–300 words; one image |
| Newsletter item | 100–200 words; headline and one link |
| Lay summary for a journal | Journal's headings and word limit; reading age about 12–14 |
| Press release (with the institution's press office) | Inverted pyramid; quote from the researcher; embargo dates |

## 8. Accessibility

Alt text for images; descriptive link text; short paragraphs; headings; plain words.

## 9. Credits and permissions

Name co-authors and funders; link the paper; respect embargoes; use only images you have rights to (the authors' originals, or open-licensed figures with attribution).
